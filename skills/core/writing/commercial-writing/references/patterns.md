# Commercial transformation patterns

All example amounts, outcomes and commitments are fictional. Replace them only
with supported facts for the actual recipient; do not borrow them as promises.

## 1. Conditional opportunity

Weak:

> Eligibility for potential support should be investigated further.

Strong:

> **Up to [amount] may cover [scope].** By [date], [owner] will confirm eligibility with [authority], assemble [documents], and protect the option before commitment.

Use only when the program, amount, and route have credible support. Say “may,” “up to,” or “if approved” until eligibility is confirmed; never imply secured money.

## 2. Missing numbers

Weak:

> More analysis is needed before the costs can be understood.

Strong:

> **Get the three-year cost comparison in ten working days.** Provide the last twelve months of invoices, internal handling time, and expected implementation costs. The result will show the base case, downside, upside, and payback range.

Use a time commitment only when it is realistic. Otherwise state the exact work package without inventing a deadline.

## 3. Weak value proposition

Weak:

> A comprehensive platform that streamlines workflows and enables teams to work more efficiently.

Strong:

> **Close the monthly books in two days instead of five.** One reconciliation queue replaces spreadsheet handoffs, exposes every exception, and gives finance a signed audit trail.

The exact outcome and mechanism matter more than broad positive adjectives.

## 4. Earned status

Empty praise:

> As an innovative and forward-thinking organization, you are leading the market.

Earned reinforcement:

> With this decision, the team converts six months of operating evidence into a controlled rollout—and keeps pricing, scope, and supplier choice in its own hands.

Derive the attractive identity from the decision, preparation, or control actually present.

## 5. Decision ask

Weak:

> We would welcome the opportunity to discuss possible next steps.

Strong:

> **Approve the two-week assessment by Friday.** On day ten, the steering group receives the cost model, bounded pilot scope, delivery risks, and a go/no-go recommendation.

Name the action, owner or audience, timing, and result.

## 6. Page hierarchy

Weak first page:

1. document type
2. abstract title
3. long context paragraph
4. methodology
5. disclaimers
6. value later

Commercial first page:

1. outcome, number, or decision
2. one-sentence mechanism
3. two or three proof signals
4. concrete next action
5. necessary condition or status note

Template:

> **[Outcome or quantified value] for [audience]**
>
> [One sentence explaining the mechanism and differentiation.]
>
> **Next:** [specific decision or action] to achieve [result].
>
> [Proof 1] · [Proof 2] · [Proof 3]
