## 1. Claim discipline

### Verification triad

Before publishing a consequential claim about price, savings, revenue, funding, performance, eligibility, timing, law, compliance, or a binding commitment, answer three separate questions:

1. **Source:** Does a current primary source or user-approved record support it?
2. **Applicability:** Does it apply to this audience, period, scope, geography, product, and scenario?
3. **Control:** Do the number, date, calculation, and wording survive an independent check?

For claims with high downside, add another authoritative source or domain review. Three derivative secondary sources do not create independent verification.

### Publication rule

- All three checks pass: state the claim clearly.
- Source passes but applicability remains conditional: state the opportunity with the condition next to it.
- Source or calculation fails: exclude the unsupported promise from public copy. Preserve an unresolved status or limitation when the reader needs it to decide.
- A material legal or commercial limitation exists: state it once, plainly, near the relevant claim.

Do not confuse confidence of tone with confidence of evidence.

## 2. Commercial value map

Identify which value dimensions actually matter to the audience:

| Dimension | Useful evidence | Common mistake |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | conversion, volume, price, retention | treating interest as booked revenue |
| Cost | invoices, labor, defects, rework | counting the same saving twice |
| Time | cycle time, wait time, throughput | calling all released time cash savings |
| Risk | probability, impact, control | selling fear without a credible mechanism |
| Control | ownership, portability, optionality | forcing strategic value into fake ROI |
| Status | leadership, confidence, credibility | generic praise or unsupported superlatives |
| Opportunity | market access, learning, reuse | listing possibilities unrelated to the decision |

Choose one primary value and at most two supporting values. More benefits do not automatically create a stronger case.

## 3. Money and business-case logic

### Build the model

Capture only relevant categories:

- current recurring cost
- current hidden or internal cost
- one-time change cost
- future recurring cost
- implementation and adoption cost
- avoided cost
- incremental revenue or contribution
- financing, discount, or conditional subsidy
- time horizon and residual value
- downside, base, and upside assumptions

Use transparent formulas:

`Current TCO(n) = current one-time costs + n × current annual run rate`

`Future TCO(n) = change costs + n × future annual run rate + retained costs - confirmed financing`

`Net difference(n) = Current TCO(n) - Future TCO(n) + verified incremental contribution`

Do not subtract conditional financing in the base case. Show separate scenarios. Label estimates, assumptions, and ranges.

### Classify value correctly

- A bill no longer paid is cash savings.
- Released labor is capacity until its use or avoided hire is demonstrated.
- Faster work may improve service or throughput without reducing spend.
- Lower infrastructure cost rarely proves the full case by itself.
- Strategic control, trust, and switching power may be valuable without a fabricated currency value.

## 4. Commercial initiative

### Three-move rule

For each material gap, prepare:

1. **Resolution:** identify the authoritative source, owner, or dataset.
2. **Execution:** draft the request, calculation, test, or evidence package.
3. **Unlock:** show the decision, claim, or opportunity that becomes available.

### Opportunity search

Consider, when relevant:

- a more compelling segment or use case
- a smaller paid first step
- a stronger bundle, guarantee, or risk reversal
- a financing, partnership, or procurement route
- existing spend that can be redirected
- proof that serves both sales and risk reduction
- reusable components, distribution, or expansion

Include an adjacent opportunity only when it improves the current decision.

### Earned status reinforcement

Make the audience feel competent, prepared, in control, or ahead for a factual reason. Tie status to an action or asset:

- a well-timed decision,
- disciplined control of downside,
- existing preparation,
- a defensible leadership opportunity,
- increased choice or bargaining power.

Do not label the audience “innovative,” “visionary,” or “forward-thinking” without evidence.

## 5. Decision architectures

### Commercial proposal or business case

1. decision and primary outcome
2. value at stake
3. why now
4. baseline versus proposed path
5. bounded first commitment
6. proof and controlled risks
7. owner, action, and date

### Landing page

1. outcome and audience
2. mechanism or differentiation
3. economic, time, or proof signal
4. cost of the current state
5. offer and how it works
6. evidence
7. objections
8. specific CTA and expected result

### Executive memo or commercial email

1. conclusion
2. economic or practical effect
3. two to four supporting facts
4. explicit request and timing

### Pitch or deck

1. tension or opportunity
2. quantified stakes
3. distinctive insight
4. offer or solution
5. proof
6. business model or economics
7. ask and next milestone

### Existing long-form draft

1. label the function of each section,
2. merge repeated functions,
3. move value and proof forward,
4. translate internal process language into audience outcomes,
5. move non-decision detail to a later layer or appendix.

## 6. Language and conditions

Unslop owns the common language rules. Use customer language and keep the ask
proportionate to the supported offer. State a necessary condition once beside
the claim it limits. If approval, applicability or delivery remains unresolved,
keep that status visible wherever removing it would mislead the reader.
Keep internal research steps out of audience-facing copy unless the recipient
needs them to act. Concision must preserve the basis of the decision.

## 7. Visual hierarchy

Prioritize:

1. outcome, number, or decision
2. explanatory subline
3. two or three proof points
4. next action
5. detail, method, and sources

Rules:

- Put the largest commercial lever above the first scroll or page break.
- Give exact comparisons a table, not competing prose.
- Give each page or section one dominant idea.
- Keep sources visible but subordinate to the decision path.
- Make the CTA name an action and its result, not “learn more” or “contact us.”
- Test hierarchy in the rendered artifact, not only in source text.

## 8. Quality score

Score each dimension 0, 1, or 2:

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Commercial job | unclear | inferable | immediately clear |
| Value | absent | mentioned | prioritized and specific |
| Evidence | unsupported | partial | applicable and checked |
| Initiative | questions only | some steps | three moves ahead |
| Structure | report-like | readable | decision-oriented |
| Voice | generic/AI | mixed | natural and distinctive |
| Hierarchy | flat | partly prioritized | value is visually dominant |
| CTA | vague | concrete | low-friction action with outcome |

Target at least 13/16. Never accept 0 for Evidence. Rebuild rather than polish when Commercial job or CTA scores 0.
